What students learn

  • Tell the difference between a tool that always does the same thing and an AI system that finds patterns and makes guesses.
  • Explain that people build AI systems and choose the examples they are built from.
  • Show how an AI system can produce something that sounds right and is still wrong, and say how a person can check.
  • Describe AI in precise words: it sorts, predicts and guesses from patterns; it is not a person.
  • Decide when to ask an adult, and what should happen to the information they share with apps and devices.
  • Match their own senses to the sensors that let a machine take in light, sound and touch.

Five threads

The same five threads come back in every age band, a step further each time.

What AI is (and isn't)

Spot AI in everyday life, and tell a tool that follows fixed steps from one that guesses from patterns.

How AI learns

People choose the examples, and the system finds patterns in them. More, more varied and more correct examples give better guesses.

When AI is wrong (and how we check)

AI can sound sure and still be wrong. Students practise checking, and ask who could be missed.

AI-made content

Pictures, texts and voices can be made with AI. Students look for clues and ask why it matters who made something.

Me and AI

What apps and devices do with what we share, who decides what we see, and when to ask an adult.

How a lesson works

  • Every lesson has a teacher page you can read in about ten minutes, and a student PDF to print.
  • Students work on their own first, then in pairs, and then the whole class pulls it together: individual, pairs, all.
  • You lead, and the students do most of the work. Suggested wording helps where the words matter, for example to describe what a machine does without making it sound like a person.
  • Every lesson ends with a closing question that each student answers before leaving.
  • The lessons work on their own or in order. Pick what suits your class.

What you need

  • A printer. The student PDFs are A4 and fit US Letter. Most print well in black and white; when a page needs colour, the teacher page says so.
  • A board, and a projector for the lessons with a short teacher demo of an AI tool.
  • No devices or accounts for students. In this track, children never use AI themselves.
  • About ten minutes to read the teacher page, plus the preparation it lists.
