How to read these numbers

  • "Months of progress" is a teacher-friendly shorthand from the EEF Toolkit. It is not directly comparable across studies — different meta-analyses use different baselines, age groups, and outcome measures. Treat it as a magnitude indicator, not a precise prediction.
  • Cohen's d is the standardised effect size used in the original meta-analyses. d ≈ 0.40 is Hattie's "hinge point" — the average effect of a year of schooling. Higher = larger relative effect, but context matters more than the number.
  • Effect sizes are averages. A skill that shows large average effects can still produce small or negative effects in a specific classroom. Use these as a starting point for professional judgment, not a substitute for it.
  • Sources are dated. Where multiple meta-analyses exist, we lead with the most recent quality study and cross-reference EEF where available.
  • Implementation cost (Low / Medium / High) is a practical signal — not an exact science — of what your school needs to invest in teacher time, training, and structural changes to actually run the intervention well. It is the editorial team's reading of what the intervention typically requires in practice. Use it to gauge whether something is straightforward to introduce or a larger undertaking — not as a budget figure.
  • Some interventions are also priced in £ (UK) by the EEF Toolkit. For monetary cost data, see the EEF Teaching & Learning Toolkit.
  • Colour bands signal calibration, not value. An intervention in the "below typical" band is not "bad" — it means the intervention's average effect is below the typical effect of a year of schooling. That can still be appropriate for specific contexts the average does not capture.

Belonging & Classroom Culture Designer

Design classroom culture so every student — including the ones who feel most on the edge — has reliable signals that they belong here, are seen as capable, and will be supported when things get hard. Relational routines, repair after conflict, identity-safe materials, and explicit anti-marginalisation moves rather than 'be kind' posters.

Motivation Diagnostic & Task Redesign

Diagnose which of the three Self-Determination Theory needs — autonomy, competence, relatedness — is undermet for a student or a class, then redesign the task to address it: meaningful choices, calibrated challenge, explicit rationale, and connection to peers' work instead of vague 'engagement' tweaks.

Self-Efficacy Builder Sequence

Build a sequence that grows real self-efficacy through Bandura's four sources — calibrated mastery experiences, modelling, social persuasion, and affective scaffolding — with attribution-style feedback that points students at effort and strategy, not innate ability. Pep-talk alone does not move the needle.

Agency Scaffold Generator

Build scaffolds that move students from teacher-directed to self-directed — meaningful choice structures, voice routines that actually get acted on, and a gradual release sequence (modelling, guided practice, independent work) instead of throwing them into the deep end and calling it autonomy.

Restorative Practice Protocol Designer

Design restorative protocols — community-building circles, restorative conferences, peer mediation, repair conversations — that address harm and rebuild relationships instead of just punishing, while keeping accountability visible. Skip the 'feelings circle' caricature: real RP is structured, named, and time-bounded.
