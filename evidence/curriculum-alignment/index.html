Curriculum Alignment 0 interventions

Tools for the work of coordinating what is taught against what is required and what is assessed. Coverage audits, framework crosswalks, KUD (Know/Understand/Do) chart authoring, and developmental band translation. The evidence question for this domain is different from intervention domains: alignment doesn't teach students — it helps the school's teaching reach what it intends to teach. The evidence base is methodological (Webb's Depth of Knowledge, Porter's content-analysis indices, Wiggins & McTighe's backward design, Squires' alignment scholarship) rather than pooled-effect-size; the value of these tools lies in process discipline and audit transparency, not in a per-student Cohen's d.

How to read these numbers

  • "Months of progress" is a teacher-friendly shorthand from the EEF Toolkit. It is not directly comparable across studies — different meta-analyses use different baselines, age groups, and outcome measures. Treat it as a magnitude indicator, not a precise prediction.
  • Cohen's d is the standardised effect size used in the original meta-analyses. d ≈ 0.40 is Hattie's "hinge point" — the average effect of a year of schooling. Higher = larger relative effect, but context matters more than the number.
  • Effect sizes are averages. A skill that shows large average effects can still produce small or negative effects in a specific classroom. Use these as a starting point for professional judgment, not a substitute for it.
  • Sources are dated. Where multiple meta-analyses exist, we lead with the most recent quality study and cross-reference EEF where available.
  • Implementation cost (Low / Medium / High) is a practical signal — not an exact science — of what your school needs to invest in teacher time, training, and structural changes to actually run the intervention well. It is the editorial team's reading of what the intervention typically requires in practice. Use it to gauge whether something is straightforward to introduce or a larger undertaking — not as a budget figure.
  • Some interventions are also priced in £ (UK) by the EEF Toolkit. For monetary cost data, see the EEF Teaching & Learning Toolkit.
  • Colour bands signal calibration, not value. An intervention in the "below typical" band is not "bad" — it means the intervention's average effect is below the typical effect of a year of schooling. That can still be appropriate for specific contexts the average does not capture.
  • Curriculum-alignment skills are tools, not interventions. "No independent meta-analysis found" for several cards in this domain reflects that the underlying alignment scholarship (Webb, Porter, Wiggins, McTighe, Squires) is methodological — it studies what alignment looks like and how to do it, not pooled student outcomes from using one specific authoring tool. The value of these tools is process discipline and audit transparency for curriculum coordinators.

Coverage Audit

Take a curriculum framework and a statutory or accreditation requirement list; produce a coverage table, gap summary, and CSV showing which framework content covers each requirement and where gaps exist. The audit itself doesn't teach — it surfaces what is and isn't covered, so the school can decide what to add, drop, or sequence differently. The evidence behind doing this work lives in Opportunity-to-Learn research: Schmidt, Guo & Houang (2021) found in US PISA 2012 mathematics data that OTL's effect on achievement was approximately 4× the effect of socioeconomic status; the TIMSS curriculum analysis tradition (Schmidt & Houang and colleagues across multiple cycles) consistently finds positive associations between coverage and achievement; Squires (2012) reviewed alignment research and reported consistent benefits when written, taught, and tested curriculum align. None of this generates a Cohen's d for the audit itself — the audit is a tool, and the gain materialises only if the school acts on what it reveals.

Curriculum Crosswalk

Compare two or more band-tagged curriculum frameworks and produce a framework-neutral topic matrix showing coverage and gaps across all inputs, plus an optional reference-centric Professional Learning Community crosswalk document when a reference framework is supplied. Anchored on the methodological scholarship of curriculum alignment: Webb's Depth of Knowledge (1997, 2007) for cognitive-complexity coding, Porter's alignment index (2002) for the P in [0,1] agreement metric between any two coded curricula, Hess's Cognitive Rigor Matrix (2004–2012) for combining Bloom's revised taxonomy with Webb's DoK, and Anderson & Krathwohl (2001) for the cognitive-process dimensions. These methodological frameworks are how alignment gets measured; the crosswalk applies them. Verification status: shipped unverified because a crosswalk is a methodological tool, not an intervention — the upstream evidence (Schmidt, Guo & Houang 2021's OTL finding, the TIMSS curriculum-coverage tradition) addresses why alignment matters, not what a specific crosswalk procedure produces. Source-voice preservation is built into the methodology: the matrix records each framework's own labels rather than rewriting them.
